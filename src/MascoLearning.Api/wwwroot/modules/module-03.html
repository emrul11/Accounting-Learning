Subledger vs General Ledger

"Why track H&M's debt invoice-by-invoice AND show one single total to the bank — and what happens when the two disagree?"

The Mystery

Sunday, 9 AM. The bank calls Shirin: "Your receivable statement shows ৳8.2 crore outstanding. We need the exact figure for your loan review." Shirin checks the General Ledger: Accounts Receivable = ৳8.2 crore. Good.

Then Nasrin storms in: "H&M's portal says we have billed them ৳3.4 crore and they've paid everything except ৳60 lakh — but our buyer-wise sheet shows ৳75 lakh pending!" The AR subledger's buyer rows sum to ৳8.5 crore, not ৳8.2 crore.

Two numbers. Both official. ৳30 lakh apart. Which one is right — and what does the difference actually mean? By the end of this module you'll not only answer, you'll know which number the bank should never have been shown first.

The GL keeps one line per concept (Module 2's cabinet). Subledgers keep the thousands of rows underneath — per buyer, per supplier, per invoice. When the layers disagree, the difference IS the finding.

The Lesson: Summary and Detail, On Purpose

Here's the tension. H&M places hundreds of orders a year through dozens of invoices, paying some partially, disputing others. The commercial team needs that invoice-level story daily. But the Balance Sheet cannot have 400 lines of buyers — it needs ONE line: "people owe us ৳8.2 crore."

Accounting's answer: keep BOTH, in two layers.

  • General Ledger (GL): the single source of truth for totals. One account per concept — 1310 Receivable—Export. This is what feeds financial statements.
  • Subledger: the detail layer beneath a GL control account — one row per buyer, per invoice: H&M invoice #INV-8891, ৳12,40,000, paid ৳10,00,000 on 12 Aug, open ৳2,40,000.

The GL account that carries a subledger underneath is called a control account. Its one job: equal the sum of its subledger rows at every moment.

The sacred rule: reconciliation

Subledger total must equal control account balance — every day, not just at month-end. When they differ (like our ৳8.5 vs ৳8.2 crore), the difference is not "rounding". It is a broken posting: something entered one layer but not the other.

And here is the rule that surprises people:

When subledger and GL disagree, the SUBLEDGER is usually right. The GL is a summary that can miss or double entries; the subledger is built from the actual documents (invoices, GRNs, payments). You fix the GL by finding the missing detail — never by typing a "balancing" journal to force them equal. Forcing equality hides the real error and destroys the audit trail.

Where Masco needs subledgers

  • AR subledger — buyer-wise: H&M, Zara, C&A… invoice-level, feeding aging reports.
  • AP subledger — supplier-wise: ABC Yarn, Delta Textile… LC-wise even.
  • Inventory subledgers — item-wise quantity & value (yarn lot 2026-C-118, 5,000 kg…).
  • Fixed asset register — machine-by-machine, feeding depreciation (Module 10).
There are no dumb questions
Is the subledger part of "the accounting system" or separate?
Fully part of it — in the ERP they are tables in the same database, written in the SAME transaction as the GL entry. The danger in old manual systems was keeping them in different notebooks. In software, one event = one GL entry + its subledger rows, atomically.
Can I fix a mismatch by posting a small journal to the GL?
Almost never. A manual journal can make totals LOOK equal, but it doesn't attach to any document, so the underlying truth stays broken and the next mismatch confuses everyone. Find the missing/ duplicated posting instead.
Why did the bank get told ৳8.2 crore if the detail says ৳8.5?
That's the mystery we're solving — and the uncomfortable answer is: until reconciled, NEITHER number should be presented as final. The ৳30 lakh gap must be explained first. (The quiz will make you do exactly that.)

  3. Subledger — this is the aging report, built from detail rows.
  4. GL — statements show the control account only.
Exercise 2 — The ৳30 lakh gap

List three possible causes for subledger ৳8.5Cr vs GL ৳8.2Cr, and say which document trail you'd check for each. Write before revealing!

Solution to Exercise 2:

  1. A payment posted to the GL but never applied to buyer rows (bank statement vs payment application screen).
  2. A manual GL journal that skipped the subledger (journal listing with no document reference — Module 0's Golden Rule alarm!).
  3. Duplicate invoice rows in the subledger or an invoice posted in detail but its GL batch failed (invoice register vs GL posting log, matched by document number).

Notice: every fix starts from DOCUMENTS, never from "adjust the GL until it matches."
