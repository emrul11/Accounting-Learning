Financial Statements

"Three documents walk into a boardroom. One says you're wise, one says you're rich, one says you can eat. Which is which?"

The Mystery

Year-end board meeting. Three papers circulate:

Paper 1 shows Masco earned ৳12 crore profit.
Paper 2 shows cash INCREASED by only ৳40 lakh.
Paper 3 shows the company now owns ৳210 crore of stuff.

A director asks the question this entire site has been building toward: "Are these three documents telling ONE story or THREE?"

One story. Three cameras filming it from different angles. By the end of this module you'll be able to hold all three in your head at once — the moment accounting truly 'clicks' for most people.

The Lesson: Three Cameras, One Story

Camera 1 — Income Statement (the period's wisdom)

Takes every REVENUE and every EXPENSE account (COGS, salaries, depreciation, FX losses…), nets them, and answers: did value created exceed value consumed? Built directly from Module 6's matching: B/L-dated revenues against the same shipments' COGS.

Export revenue (net)৳142,00,00,000
− COGS(115,00,00,000)
= Gross profit27,00,00,000 (19%)
− Admin + selling + FX + other(11,50,00,000)
= Operating profit → after finance costs/tax৳12,00,00,000 net

Camera 2 — Balance Sheet (the moment's truth)

Freezes ONE instant and lists Assets = Liabilities + Equity. Every account from Module 2 lands here sorted by type. The equation ALWAYS holds because Module 1's double-entry never let it wobble.

Cash + receivables + inventory + machines (book values)ASSETS ৳210,00,00,000
LC payables + loans + VAT/TDS + provisionsLIABILITIES ৳138,00,00,000
Share capital + retained profits (incl. THIS year's 12Cr)EQUITY 72,00,00,000

Camera 3 — Cash Flow Statement (the survival report)

Reconciles opening cash to closing cash across three activities: Operating (business heartbeat), Investing (buying/selling machines), Financing (loans, shares, dividends). It explains WHY ৳12 crore profit became only ৳40 lakh extra cash: profit got parked inside growing receivables and inventory (Module 9!), depreciation added back (Module 10 — no cash left), loan repayments drained financing.

The Secret That Binds Them: Net profit from Camera 1 flows INTO Equity on Camera 2 (retained earnings). And Camera 3 starts from that same profit, then undoes every non-cash timing to explain real cash movement. Break any single entry anywhere upstream, and all three cameras go subtly wrong TOGETHER. That's why auditors love reconciling them — inconsistencies between cameras expose errors like floodlights.
There are no dumb questions
Which statement matters MOST?
Wrong question — they answer different questions for different decisions. Banks read balance sheet + cash flow; buyers of the company read profit trends; the MD needs all three simultaneously. A statement used alone is a camera used without the others: easy to misdirect.
Why does depreciation appear on the cash flow statement if it isn't cash?
Because it WAS subtracted in profit. The CF statement ADDS IT BACK to show operating cash generation — then separately shows machine PURCHASES under investing. Depreciation's whole Module 10 mystery, resolved in one line here.
Who reads these besides management?
Banks (loan covenants), NBR (tax base), buyers doing factory compliance scoring, insurers, investors — and your ERP must generate all three, audit-ready, on demand.

Exercise 2 — Trace the flow

Starting from "INV-8891 ships, B/L 15 Aug": name which statements eventually feel this event, in order, and through WHAT accounts.

Solution:

  1. Immediately: AR subledger row + GL (1310↑, 4100↑, COGS↑, FG↓) → August Income Statement feels revenue & COGS; 31-Aug Balance Sheet carries remaining receivable & lower FG.
  2. Late Oct: settlement converts receivable→bank (BS composition shifts); realized FX hits P&L; October cash flow records the collection in OPERATING.
  3. Year-end: the profit contribution rolls into retained earnings within EQUITY. One shipment, three statements — one story.

Worked Example: How ৳12 Crore Profit Became ৳40 Lakh Cash

Net profit (Income Statement)৳12,00,00,000
+ Depreciation added back (non-cash)+2,70,00,000
− Receivables grew during year(9,80,00,000)
− Inventory grew during year(6,20,00,000)
+ Payables grew during year+4,30,00,000
= Cash from OPERATIONS3,00,00,000
Investing: new machines purchased(2,60,00,000)
Financing: loan repaid (net)0
NET CHANGE IN CASH৳40,00,000 ✓

Read it slowly — this IS the MD's mystery solved. Growth consumed the profit as working capital (receivables+inventory up ৳16 crore!), depreciation was never cash anyway, and machines ate more. Healthy, growing — and cash-hungry. Now imagine showing the bank THIS table with next year's plan. That's a professional conversation.

Growth warning label: fast-growing RMG factories die of this exact table. Pricing and expansion decisions MUST include the working-capital bill, not just margin.
