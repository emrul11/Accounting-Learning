Audit & Controls

"Why can't the person who CREATES a payment also APPROVE it — even if they're brilliant, loyal, and never once made a mistake?"

The Mystery

A supplier's driver arrives at the gate with "urgent invoice" and a friendly smile: "Sir, payment today please, otherwise yarn supply stops tomorrow." The storekeeper, wanting to help, approves the payment himself in the system — he's authorized on both screens. Money leaves. Everyone relaxes.

Three months later, external audit finds that this same "urgent" supplier invoiced Masco twice for one delivery, and the second payment went to an account with a suspiciously similar name. Loss: ৳18 lakh. The storekeeper is devastated — he never stole anything. He just held two keys at once.

Controls exist not because people are bad, but because ONE honest person holding every key removes all safety nets. This module designs those keys.

The Lesson: Keys, Not Heroes

Control #1 — Segregation of Duties (the big one)

No single person may hold two of these three for any flow: authorize / execute / record. The storekeeper's tragedy: he executed AND approved. Classic dangerous pairs our ERP must BLOCK: create vendor + approve payments; receive goods + post counts + adjust inventory; payroll setup + bank transfer release.

Control #2 — Approval limits & maker-checker

Amounts escalate through hands: GRN approval by storekeeper; payment up to ৳5L by manager; ৳5–50L needs finance head; beyond, MD + board matrix. Maker-checker means EVERY master-data change (new vendor bank account!) needs a second pair of eyes — vendor bank-account fraud is the most common RMG scam there is.

Control #3 — The immutable audit trail

Module 0's Golden Rule meets software: entries are never edited or deleted — corrected by REVERSALS. Every action stores who/when/ what-before/what-after. If it isn't logged, it didn't happen; if it happened without a document, it shouldn't have happened.

Control #4 — Reconciliation as detective control

Preventive controls stop errors entering; DETECTIVE controls find what slipped through: subledger-vs-GL daily (Module 3), bank reconciliation weekly-to-paisa, inventory cycle counts, payroll-vs- production cross-checks (Module 8), asset verification (Module 10).

There are no dumb questions
We're only ~1,275 people — can't we trust each other?
Trust people; distrust UNVERIFIED SYSTEMS. Good controls protect honest people from suspicion AND temptation — the storekeeper would give anything for the system that refused his double-key moment. Fraud statistics overwhelmingly show opportunity, not evil genius, as root cause.
Doesn't maker-checker slow everything down?
It adds minutes to transactions worth lakhs. One fraudulent payment costs more than ten thousand slow ones. Also: good workflow design makes checking a 10-second tap, not a meeting.
The auditor asked for 'SOC evidence' — what satisfies them?
System-generated reports: user-role listings, approval-limit configs, sample transaction trails showing document→approval→entry chains, reconciliation logs. Your ERP should produce audit evidence as REPORTS, not archaeology.

Your Turn

Exercise 1 — Spot the toxic pair

Which role combinations must NEVER sit with one person?

  1. Create vendors + approve vendor payments
  2. Receive goods + approve GRNs (same person)
  3. Run payroll file + release bank transfers
  4. Post inventory adjustments + physical stock keeping

Solutions:

  1. Toxic — classic ghost-vendor factory.
  2. Toxic — receiving must be checked by QC/another storekeeper; self-approval hides short deliveries.
  3. Toxic — file + release = entire payroll fraud capability.
  4. Toxic — custodian adjusting own stock erases theft detection forever.

All four! Segregation failures are everywhere once you look.

Exercise 3 — Guided control test

A new vendor bank account is entered by one employee and a ৳40L payment is requested the same day. Name two preventive checks and one detective check.

Solution to Exercise 3:

Preventive: require independent maker-checker approval of the bank change and block the requester from approving the payment. Detective: reconcile the vendor master, payment log, and bank statement, then investigate any unmatched change.

Exercise 4 — Independent exception

An emergency override bypassed the normal limit. What evidence must remain, who should review it, and why must the original action stay visible?

Solution to Exercise 4:

Keep the request, reason, approver, timestamp, amount, source documents, and later review result. An independent finance or internal-audit reviewer checks it promptly. Never delete or edit the original event; visibility lets the control be tested.

Exercise 2 — Design the limit matrix

Draft approval limits for purchase payments at Masco: roles = Executive, Manager, Finance Head, MD. Justify thresholds (amounts are yours to propose).

Sample solution:

  • Executive: up to ৳2L (routine trims/local buys)
  • Manager: up to ৳10L (regular raw material lots)
  • Finance Head: up to ৳50L (LC settlements)
  • MD (+board visibility): above ৳50L / any NEW vendor's first payment / any policy exception

Principles: limits match VALUE RISK, new-vendor first payments always escalate (fraud window!), exceptions always visible upward.

Worked Example: Anatomy of a Blocked Fraud

Day 9:45 AM"ABC Yarn Ltd." emails: bank account changed, please update urgently (fake domain, one letter off)
9:52 AMExecutive creates vendor-bank change request in ERP — status PENDING CHECKER
10:15 AMFinance checker calls supplier on OLD known number: no such request. REJECTED. Audit trail stores attempt + rejector.
Weekly reportException report lists ALL rejected changes; monthly review spots pattern targeting three factories

Count the controls that fired: maker-checker (no single-key changes), out-of-band verification, immutable log, exception reporting. Remove ANY ONE and ৳18 lakh walks out. This is why controls are layered — each assumes another might fail.

Predict it

Same attack, but the checker is ON LEAVE and the executive holds a temporary "checker" grant "just for today." What single control- bypass decision just multiplied the risk, and what SHOULD happen instead?

Answer:

Temporary dual-role grants destroy segregation exactly when attackers strike (they watch leave calendars). Correct pattern: DEPUTY checkers pre-designated per role; emergency access via time-boxed, logged, post-reviewed elevation — never casual role sharing.
