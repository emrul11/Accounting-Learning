The Lesson: Aging Is Everything

A receivable is a promise with a birthday. Every day it ages, three risks grow: buyer forgetting, disputes hardening, and (for exports) the Bangladesh Bank realization window closing. So Masco reads its AR not as one number but as an aging ladder:

BucketMeaningAction owner
0–30 daysHealthy — within termsNobody (yet)
31–60 daysWatch — statement check, soft reminderNasrin / commercial
61–90 daysEscalate — buyer call, dispute hunt, document re-checkCommercial + Finance jointly
90+ daysDanger — regulatory window risk (exports); provision reviewFinance + MD

The payable mirror

Payables have the same structure inverted — and a harder edge: missing a supplier payment damages trust, credit terms, and future pricing. Professional AP runs on three-way matching BEFORE payment:

  • Purchase order — what we agreed to buy, at what price
  • GRN — proof it arrived and passed QC (approved!)
  • Supplier invoice — their claim for money

All three agree → schedule payment by due date. Any mismatch → hold, query, resolve. Paying unmatched invoices is how factories fund other people's mistakes.

Cash flow forecasting = promising arithmetic

Weekly, Shirin lays expected collections (from AR aging + buyer payment patterns) against scheduled payments (AP maturities + payroll + LC settlements). The output — weeks of positive or negative gap — is called a cash flow forecast, and it is why banks extend limits to some factories and not others.

There are no dumb questions
We're profitable — how could cash run out?
Profit is earned when promises are MADE (B/L date); cash arrives when they're KEPT. Fast growth actually worsens cash: more shipments = more working capital locked in receivables and inventory. Growth financed carelessly kills more RMG factories than losses do.
Shouldn't we just delay ALL supplier payments to keep cash?
That trades tomorrow's terms for today's comfort. Suppliers respond with higher prices, stricter advance demands, and priority given to better customers. Reliability IS a financial asset.
What's a 'provision for doubtful debts'?
An honest admission: some aged receivables won't convert. We estimate the likely loss (aging-based percentages), book it as expense NOW, and write off only with evidence later (Module 13's claims/disputes). Optimism is not an accounting policy.

Exercise 3 — Guided three-way match

A supplier invoice says 1,000 kg at ৳620/kg. The approved PO says 1,000 kg at ৳620, but the GRN records 900 kg accepted. Should AP pay now? State the evidence and next action.

Solution to Exercise 3:

Start with the accepted quantity: 900 × ৳620 = ৳5,58,000. The supplier claims 1,000 × ৳620 = ৳6,20,000, leaving ৳62,000 disputed. Hold approval of the full invoice and check receiving/QC evidence. Obtain an agreed correction or credit note before settling the accepted amount under the payment terms. The remaining 100 kg must not be recorded as accepted inventory merely to make the invoice match.

Exercise 4 — Independent cash forecast

This week’s expected collections are ৳18L and scheduled payments are supplier AP ৳11L, payroll ৳4L, and LC settlement ৳5L. Calculate the net cash gap and name one responsible response.

Solution to Exercise 4:

Payments total ৳20L, so the forecast gap is ৳2L negative (18L − 20L). Finance should escalate early, verify collection dates, and negotiate an approved timing change rather than silently delaying a supplier.

Exercise 2 — Break the match

Supplier invoice arrives: ৳18,00,000 for fabric. GRN approved for ৳18,00,000 — but PO says ৳17,50,000. What does three-way matching conclude, and who resolves what?

Solution:

MISMATCH on price (invoice vs PO). Payment is HELD. Resolution path: procurement queries the supplier — was there an approved price amendment (then update PO reference) or a billing error (then credit note)? Only a matched trio releases the invoice to the payment calendar. Never "pay now, sort later" — that sentence is where margins die quietly.

From Lesson to Software

Before you take the quiz, write the software specification a developer would need to build the posting logic from this module. Predict first, then reveal the model answer.

1. What event triggers this posting?

An AP/AR lifecycle event: an invoice received from a supplier, an invoice issued to a customer, a payment received, a payment made, or a credit/debit note raised against an existing invoice. Every event is a real document in the system, not an email or a phone call.

2. Which accounts, amounts, and dates apply?

The AP or AR subledger party account on one side, and the matching account on the other — bank, cash, inventory, revenue, or VAT. Amounts come from the invoice or payment voucher. Due dates come from the agreed payment terms (e.g. 30 days from invoice date), and they drive aging.

3. What approval and validation are required?

For AP: three-way match (PO + GRN + invoice), duplicate-invoice check against invoice number and supplier, and an approved vendor. For AR: credit-limit check against the customer master, an approved customer, and a delivery note matched to the shipment. VAT and TDS rules apply where relevant.

4. What happens on cancellation, correction, or retry?

Use debit/credit notes for value corrections (a smaller amount, a price adjustment, a discount applied late). Use reversal journals for wrong account or wrong period. Every correction must reference the original document and voucher number, never overwrite the original posting.
