The Lesson: Five Shadows to Master

Shadow 1 — VAT: collected for the state, not earned by us

VAT (Value Added Tax) flows THROUGH the company. On local purchases we pay VAT Input (an asset/receivable from the state); on local sales we charge VAT Output (a liability). Net difference = monthly payment to NBR. Crucially for Masco: exports are zero-rated — no output VAT on H&M invoices, and input VAT related to exports gets refunded/offset. Recoverable input VAT is kept separate from cost. Non-recoverable VAT may form part of asset cost or expense; recovery and refund require eligibility, not merely an invoice.

Shadow 2 — Bond license & UD: duty-free has homework

The bond license lets Masco import yarn/fabric duty-free — ON THE PROMISE that inputs become EXPORTS. The Utilization Declaration (UD) proves it: imported kg consumed vs exported garments, factory by factory. Accounting's job: item-wise consumption tracking so declarations are evidence-backed (Module 0's Golden Rule serving national customs!). Waste percentages beyond permitted norms = duty recovery + penalties.

Shadow 3 — TDS & AIT: taxes withheld on the way

  • TDS (Tax Deducted at Source) — we WITHHOLD from payments to others: salaries (Module 8!), contractor bills, rent — then deposit to NBR with challans by statutory dates.
  • AIT (Advance Income Tax) — others withhold FROM us: banks deduct on LC settlements/interest; ports on imports. These are PRE-PAID tax — credits reducing year-end liability, tracked certificate-wise.

When Masco deducts tax from a supplier, it creates TDS Payable; remitting it debits that liability and credits Bank. When a customer deducts creditable tax from a payment to Masco, Masco records its own prepaid-tax asset and retains the certificate. These are separate flows: paying a withholding liability does not turn it into an asset.

Shadow 4 — Corporate tax: book profit ≠ taxable profit

Taxable income STARTS from accounting profit, then adjusts: disallowed expenses (some entertainment, penalties), different depreciation rules (tax schedules vs books — Module 10 again), concessionary export rates, minimum-tax floors regardless of losses. The reconciliation between book and taxable profit is a standing schedule your CA owns — but your ERP must keep the raw categories clean enough to build it painlessly.

Shadow 5 — WPPF: profit-sharing as law

Workers' Profit Participation Fund: statutory share of profit provisioned annually (Module 8's accrual), held in fund with trustee oversight, invested per rules. It reduces distributable profit AND carries its own compliance calendar.

⚠️ Rates change every June budget

Every rate mentioned in training materials is a snapshot with a date. NBR revises VAT/TDS/AIT/thresholds in each annual budget (typically June). Rules for implementation: never hardcode rates — config tables with effective dates, reviewed within 30 days of each budget announcement, verified at nbr.gov.bd. This module teaches STRUCTURE; current NUMBERS always come from source.

There are no dumb questions
If exports are zero-rated, does VAT not matter to Masco?
It matters enormously — local purchases, utilities, services all carry input VAT needing correct booking and refund/offset processes; and any local sales (rare for us) generate output VAT. Zero-rating changes the NET, not the discipline.
We're export-oriented — do we still file returns?
Yes. Zero-rated still means REGISTERED and FILING. Returns, UD submissions, withholding deposits, certificates — the calendar runs regardless of profitability.
What happens if TDS is deposited late?
Interest/penalty plus the deducted employee/vendor still expects their credit — meaning Masco may pay twice: once as penalty, once re-covering disputes. Deadlines here are cash, not ceremony.

Your Turn

Exercise 1 — Whose money is it?

Classify: OUR expense / STATE'S money passing through / OUR prepaid tax:

  1. VAT charged on local trims purchase (input side)
  2. TDS withheld from security agency's monthly bill
  3. AIT deducted by bank on our LC settlement proceeds
  4. Corporate tax provision for the year's profit

Solutions:

  1. OUR prepaid tax (VAT Input asset) — offsets/refunds against output obligations; for export-linked purchases, recoverable.
  2. STATE'S money passing through — we withheld it FROM the agency; liability until challan deposit.
  3. OUR prepaid tax — AIT credit reducing year-end liability, tracked by certificate.
  4. OUR expense/liability — computed from taxable income after adjustments; provisioned, then settled.
Exercise 3 — Guided ownership test

Assume a local purchase has a price before VAT of ৳1,00,000 and VAT of ৳15,000, and all conditions for full input-tax recovery are met. This is an illustrative amount, not a rate recommendation. Identify whose money the VAT is, which account receives it, and what document supports recovery.

Solution to Exercise 3:

Input VAT is a recoverable tax asset/credit, not Masco's expense or revenue. Record it separately from the purchase cost, supported by a valid VAT invoice and supplier details; finance later offsets or refunds it under the verified return.

Exercise 4 — Independent withholding

Masco owes a contractor ৳2,00,000 and withholds ৳20,000 TDS. State the payable, the liability created, and the later challan entry.

Solution to Exercise 4:

Net payable to the contractor is ৳1,80,000; credit TDS Payable ৳20,000 because Masco holds that money for NBR. When deposited, debit TDS Payable and credit Bank, retaining the challan and certificate. The withheld amount is not Masco income.

Exercise 2 — Design the rate table

Rafiq must store VAT/TDS/AIT rates for the ERP. List the columns his config table needs (hint: think budget cycles).

Solution:

  • TaxType (VAT/TDS/AIT), Category (e.g., salary, contractor, import)
  • Rate, EffectiveFrom, EffectiveTo (nullable = current)
  • SourceReference (NBR SRO/circular number), ApprovedBy, ApprovedDate

Dated rows mean June budgets become INSERTS, never code edits — and historical transactions forever show the rate THAT WAS LAW when they occurred.
