Inventory & COGS

"20,000 tees worth ৳80 lakh sit in the warehouse. When do they finally become an EXPENSE? (Only when we ship. Everything else is a trap.)"

The Mystery

Two months, same factory, same effort. In March, Masco produced garments heavily but shipped little. The trial report showed a healthy "profit". In April, production paused for Eid while shipments surged — and the report showed a terrifying "loss". The managing director demands: "Which month were we actually good or bad? And who is cooking these numbers?"

Nobody was cooking anything. But someone WAS expensing goods that hadn't sold. One rule separates real profit from inventory hallucination — and it's the most violated rule in manufacturing accounting.

The Lesson: The Matching Heartbeat

Accounting's deepest fairness principle is matching: show a sale's revenue and its cost in the SAME period, so profit is honest. For Masco that means:

  • Producing garments spends cash but creates NO expense — it just converts money from bank into inventory (asset → asset).
  • Shipping them creates revenue AND moves their accumulated cost (৳400/pc from Module 5) into Cost of Goods Sold (COGS) — in the same instant.
The One Rule: Inventory becomes COGS only when ownership passes to the buyer — for exports, on the B/L date. Produce all year without shipping and your "expense" is zero; your warehouse is simply full of assets. Expense early and you lie about losses; expense late and you lie about profits. The B/L date decides.

The perpetual rhythm (how our ERP does it)

Old factories counted shelves monthly to guess costs ("periodic"). Our ERP keeps score live ("perpetual"): every movement — GRN, issue, output, shipment — updates quantity AND value instantly via the posting engine. COGS posts automatically with rule R5 at each shipment, using the actual cost layers of the lots that left.

Which layer's cost leaves first?

Yarn arrives at different prices across the year (৳600, ৳640, ৳620…). When fabric consumes yarn, WHICH price applies? Standard answer for continuous processes like ours: moving weighted average — blend all layers into one fair average that shifts with each arrival. Simple, smooth, hard to game. (FIFO is the other legitimate choice; LIFO is not permitted under IFRS and invites games.)

There are no dumb questions
If producing doesn't create expense, why did March look "profitable"?
Because whoever prepared it expensed production costs directly instead of parking them in inventory. April then double-suffered: shipments carried costs that had ALREADY been expensed once. Both months lied in opposite directions. Correct matching makes both truthful.
Buyer rejected 500 pcs last week — is that COGS?
Careful! A rejection BEFORE shipment never left inventory — it's rework/scrap inside production. A claim AFTER shipment touches revenue and possibly COGS reversals. Different documents, different entries — Module 13 handles exactly this messiness.
What about garments damaged by monsoon leakage in the FG store?
They can never be sold as first quality — their cost must leave inventory through a WRITE-DOWN (loss), documented with photos, approval, and disposal records. Damaged stock pretending to be good stock is another classic lie.

Exercise 1 — Asset or expense?

For each moment, what is the ৳400/pc garment?

  1. Sewing machine finishes it, QC passes, it enters FG store
  2. It sits in the FG store awaiting vessel space
  3. Loaded in container, B/L issued 15 Aug, invoice raised
  4. Cash from H&M arrives 60 days later

Solutions to Exercise 1:

  1. Asset (Inventory—FG). Production finished; nothing sold yet.
  2. Still an asset. Waiting ≠ selling.
  3. Becomes COGS now — ownership passed on the B/L date; R4+R5 fire together: revenue recognized AND ৳400/pc expensed.
  4. Neither! Cash collection is just receivable→bank. Revenue and COGS happened back on the B/L date.
Exercise 2 — Catch the liar

An accountant proposes: "Shipments are paperwork-heavy. Let's expense production monthly using estimated sales percentages." Give two reasons this violates everything this site has taught.

Solution to Exercise 2:

  1. Breaks matching: estimates decouple cost from the ACTUAL shipment event — months lie in both directions (the MD mystery at the top of this page, industrialized).
  2. Breaks document-traceability: no GRN/B/L chain behind the numbers (Modules 0 and 4) — auditors find an entry with no source document, which is unforgivable by design.
