Real-World Exceptions

"The books are balanced, the rules are set… and then real life calls: short shipments, buyer claims, LC discrepancies, a dyeing machine dying mid-order. Now what?"

The Mystery

Thursday, 4 PM. Three problems arrive simultaneously:

  1. H&M claims 2,400 pcs defective from last month's shipment, demanding credit.
  2. The buyer's LC has a discrepancy: invoice says 100% cotton, the test certificate says 98% — bank refuses to pay until fixed.
  3. A dyeing machine's main pump died mid-batch; 8,000 m of fabric is half-processed and the repair quote is ৳22 lakh with three weeks lead time.

The intern panics: "Which module of the textbook covers this?!" Answer: none of them individually. All of them together. Watch every rule you've learned recombine under pressure.

The Lesson: Same Rules, Harder Days

Exception 1 — Buyer claims (defects/shortages after shipment)

Ownership passed at B/L date (Module 6), so a valid claim REVERSES part of that original event — but only through documents: claim letter, inspection/failure analysis, negotiated settlement, then a credit note reducing revenue (and AR), plus COGS adjustment for returned goods if they come back. What it never is: a silent AR edit.

Exception 2 — LC discrepancies

Banks pay against DOCUMENTS, not goods. One inconsistent letter (cotton % mismatch!) freezes cash. Resolution is documentary: amended certificate, buyer's waiver acceptance, or re-issued invoice — then the clock keeps running toward Module 7's realization window. Discrepancy days are aging days; chase them like debts, because they become exactly that.

Exception 3 — Breakdowns & idle capacity

The dead pump creates REAL costs with nowhere natural to go: emergency repairs, overtime catch-up, half-processed WIP sitting still. Accounting treatment: repair costs route to overhead (or capitalized if life-extending — Module 10's test!), idle time gets REPORTED as variance (Module 5's radar), and affected orders get re-costed honestly — not hidden inside other orders' buckets.

The meta-rule for every exception

Exceptions = normal accounting + extra evidence + explicit approval. Every exception needs its OWN document chain (claim file, discrepancy correspondence, breakdown report), an owner, and a deadline. "We'll fix it in the system somehow" is how honest factories develop mysterious balances nobody can explain by audit season.
There are no dumb questions
Can we just refuse all buyer claims to protect profit?
Refuse INVALID ones — with documented quality evidence. Refusing VALID ones converts a ৳15L claim into a lost ৳40Cr relationship. Claims handling is commercial strategy executed through accounting documents; both halves matter.
If insurance covers the machine damage, do we still record the loss?
Record reality first (damage, repair costs), THEN the insurance recovery as its own item when confirmed. Net-net may be small; gross honesty always matters — insurers and auditors both read the full story.
Who owns exceptions day-to-day?
Commercial owns claims/discrepancies (their documents caused or cure them); production+engineering own breakdown recovery; finance owns the LEDGER truth of each — tracking open exceptions weekly like Module 9's aging ladder. Unowned exceptions rot.

Your Turn

Exercise 1 — Route the exception

Name the document chain and the accounts touched:

  1. H&M's 2,400-pc claim settled as valid — they accept 12% price reduction (no return)
  2. Cotton-test certificate corrected and LC bank accepts waiver
  3. Pump repaired for ৳22L — routine restoration, no life extension beyond original

Solutions:

  1. Claim correspondence + failure analysis + settlement approval → credit note: Dr Sales Return/Discount (revenue↓) / Cr AR—H&M. No inventory movement — nothing came back.
  2. Corrected certificate + bank waiver letter → NO new entry at all! Documents cured a DOCUMENT problem; money entries were never wrong. But aging clock kept ticking throughout — chase speed mattered.
  3. Breakdown report + repair invoice → Dr Repairs & Maintenance OH / Cr Bank/AP. Routine restoration = period overhead (Module 10's capitalize-vs-expense test).
Exercise 3 — Guided changed condition

A buyer reports 100 defective pieces after the B/L date, but inspection finds only 60 valid. What documents and accounting decision are needed?

Solution to Exercise 3:

Collect the claim letter, inspection/failure analysis, shipment reference, and settlement approval. Credit only the agreed 60-piece claim through a credit note; do not silently edit AR. Assess any return and COGS reversal separately.

Exercise 4 — Independent breakdown

A pump repair costs ৳22L and restores its original capacity; it does not extend useful life. Decide expense route, evidence, and one follow-up control.

Solution to Exercise 4:

Route the routine restoration to repair expense or factory overhead under policy, supported by the work order and invoice; do not capitalize it as a life-extending asset. Reconcile downtime, repair approval, and production variance afterward.

Exercise 2 — The tempting shortcut

To keep H&M happy, someone suggests: "Don't issue a credit note; just reduce their NEXT invoice quietly." Give two reasons this violates your training.

Solution:

  1. Document trail breaks: next month's invoice would carry an invisible deduction unrelated to ITS shipment — reconciliation between Masco's books and H&M's portal becomes archaeology (Module 9 pain).
  2. Misstates BOTH periods: this quarter hides a cost belonging to last quarter's shipment; matching dies (Module 6) and claim statistics vanish (how many defects? which line? unanswerable).

From Lesson to Software

Before you take the quiz, write the software specification a developer would need to build the posting logic from this module. Predict first, then reveal the model answer.

1. What event triggers this posting?

An exception is detected: a short shipment, a return from H&M, a duplicate supplier invoice, a missing supporting document, or an exchange-rate discrepancy. Every exception is a real event that needs its own recorded correction path — never silent.

2. Which accounts, amounts, and dates apply?

Depends on the exception: a suspense account for an unknown difference, an inventory-return account for a customer return, an AP adjustment account for a price dispute. The amount is the disputed or returned value, and the date is the exception date (when the problem was found), not the original transaction date.

3. What approval and validation are required?

Finance must approve the exception. The original entry must be referenced. A supporting document must be attached, or a query note recorded explaining why one is missing. The impact on VAT, revenue recognition, and period must be identified before the fix is posted.

4. What happens on cancellation, correction, or retry?

Clear the suspense to the correct account once the root cause is known. If the whole transaction was wrong, reverse the original entry and re-post the correct one — never edit the original. Every fix is dated and linked to the exception record, so the trail from problem to resolution is one click away.
