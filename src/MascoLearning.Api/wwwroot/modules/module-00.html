How Business Becomes Accounting

"How does a truck of yarn at the factory gate turn into numbers inside a computer?"

The Mystery

Tuesday, 7:40 AM, Gazipur gate. A truck carrying 5,000 kg of cotton yarn from Shanghai enters the Masco compound. Jamal, the warehouse keeper, signs a piece of paper called a GRN. Nasrin, in the commercial office, checks one line in an LC document. Shirin, in accounts, types something into the system.

Nobody counted any money. No cash moved. Yet by lunchtime, Masco's accounting records say the company owns ৳31 lakh more inventory and owes ৳31 lakh more to a supplier.

Who turned a truck into numbers? By the end of this module, you will be able to explain exactly how.

Everything you will ever do on this learning site — and everything the new ERP accounting software will ever do — is one version or another of the journey above: a real thing happens, a paper proves it happened, and accounting records it in numbers.

The Lesson: The Three-Step Bridge

Forget debit and credit for ten minutes. Those come in Module 1. Right now you only need one idea:

Accounting is a language that translates business events into numbers. And like any translation, it follows the same three steps every single time:

1

An EVENT happens

A truck arrives. A worker is paid. A shirt is shipped.

→
2

A DOCUMENT proves it

GRN, salary sheet, B/L. Paper (or a screen) with names, dates, amounts.

→
3

An ENTRY records it

The numbers enter the accounting system and never change silently again.

Look back at Tuesday morning at the gate. The event was the yarn arriving. The document was the GRN — Goods Received Note — signed by Jamal. The entry was what appeared in the system after Shirin approved it: inventory up, supplier debt up.

Now the sentence you should tattoo on your brain:

The Golden Rule of Documents No document, no entry. If there is no proof that the event happened, the accounting system must refuse to record it. This one rule kills most fraud, most errors, and most arguments with auditors.

Why should a software developer care?

Because the ERP accounting module you are building is this bridge, automated. When Jamal clicks "Approve GRN", your code must create the correct entry. If you let an entry exist without its document reference, you have broken the Golden Rule in code form. Every table we design, every API we write, exists to serve steps 1 → 2 → 3.

Why should everyone else care?

Because your daily paperwork is step 2. When Nasrin delays checking an LC document, or Jamal creates the GRN three days late, the company's numbers are wrong for those days — and decisions get made on wrong numbers. Slow documents = lying reports.

There are no dumb questions
Is accounting just about money?
No! Notice that no money moved when the yarn arrived. Accounting tracks economic value: things you own (yarn), things you owe (supplier bills), promises made to you (H&M's LC). Money is only one kind of value.
Can't we record the yarn when someone remembers to type it?
That is how companies lose control of their own facts. If entries depend on memory or mood, two people will honestly report two different truths. Documents make truth boring, repeatable, and auditable — which is exactly what you want.
What if the document is wrong?
Then the entry is wrong, even though the process worked. That's why documents have approvers (Module 12) and why exceptions like rejected yarn have special flows (Module 13). Wrong document? Fix the document through a proper correction — never by quietly editing the numbers.
Fireside chat
The Warehouse

I saw the yarn with my own eyes. Five thousand kilos, sitting in my godown. Why do I need to wait for some office to "approve" anything before the system knows what I know?

The General Ledger

Because I don't record what you see — I record what can be proven. Your eyes are excellent, but you go home at night, and someday you will retire. The GRN stays. It says: quantity, quality-checked, agreed by both sides. When the auditor asks me in 2031 where this yarn came from, I show the GRN, not your eyes.

The Warehouse

…Fine. But then approve my GRNs quickly. My godown is honest, but it is not patient.

Your Turn

Exercise 1 — Name that step

For each situation below, decide which of the three steps it is: Event, Document, or Entry. Write your answers down before revealing.

  1. Jamal signs a GRN for 5,000 kg yarn.
  2. The system now shows ৳31 lakh more inventory.
  3. 500 shirts are loaded into the C&F van for Chittagong port.

Solutions to Exercise 1:

  1. Document. The physical event was the truck arriving; the GRN is the proof created at that moment.
  2. Entry. Numbers in the system = the third step. The bridge has been crossed.
  3. Event. Nothing has been documented yet — if nothing gets written down about this van, this event never officially happened. (That's how garments disappear.)
Exercise 2 — Spot the danger

A production supervisor tells you: "We started using a new glue supplier last month. He delivers directly to the floor and we pay him cash from the store's petty fund. No papers."

Which part of the three-step bridge is missing, and name two bad consequences.

Solution to Exercise 2:

Step 2 — the document — is missing entirely. Consequences include (any two):

  • The glue cost never becomes part of product cost, so garment costing is understated and profit is overstated (or vice versa).
  • Cash leaves the company with zero audit trail — a fraud magnet.
  • NBR will disallow the expense for tax because there is no voucher.
  • If quality fails later, there is no document to claim against the supplier.

Worked Example: Tuesday Morning, End to End

Let's finish the story properly. On Tuesday, ABC Yarn Ltd.'s truck delivered 5,000 kg of combed cotton yarn. The LC price works out to ৳620 per kg, so the shipment is worth ৳31,00,000. Payment to ABC happens later, against the LC. Watch the three steps:

Step 1 — Event Truck arrives at Gazipur gate, 7:40 AM, unloaded by 11 AM.
Step 2 — Document Jamal weighs, samples QC, creates GRN #GR-2026-0441: 5,000 kg, accepted 5,000 kg.
Step 3 — Entry Shirin approves the GRN in the system at 12:05 PM → the system posts the journal entry below automatically.
Account Debit (BDT) Credit (BDT)
Inventory — Yarn31,00,000
Accounts Payable — ABC Yarn Ltd. (LC)31,00,000
Narration: Yarn received vide GR#GR-2026-0441 against LC #8891 31,00,000 31,00,000

Don't worry yet about why inventory sits on the left (debit) and the supplier on the right (credit) — that is the whole of Module 1. Today, notice only these things:

  • The entry carries the GRN number. Document and entry are glued together forever.
  • No bank account changed. Payment day is a different event, with its own document and its own entry.
  • Nobody typed "inventory up". Approving the GRN caused the entry. That automation is Module 4's posting engine.

What This Means for Your Job

Rafiq — Developer

You build the bridge

Every entry your code writes must carry its source document ID. Design your APIs so an entry cannot exist orphaned. If someone asks for "post this adjustment without a source doc", the answer is no — build a document flow instead.

Nasrin — Commercial

Your papers are step 2

LCs, B/Ls, invoices — these are the documents that let entries exist. One late B/L check and revenue lands in the wrong month. Your desk controls when the company's truth changes.

Jamal — Warehouse

You start the bridge

Same-day GRNs are not bureaucracy — they are the difference between true and false company records. The yarn existed physically on Tuesday; the system may not say so until Thursday unless you act same-day.

Shirin — Finance

You guard step 3

You approve entries, but never without their documents. When a number looks odd, your first question is always: which document created this? Trace backwards, fix forward.

Mizan — HR/Payroll

Your salary sheet is a document too

Attendance sheets and salary sheets are step 2 for worker costs. A salary paid without a signed sheet is an un-provable expense — audit trouble and WPPF calculation errors live here.
