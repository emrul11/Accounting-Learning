Foreign Currency & Letter of Credit

"One shipment, one invoice — so why does ONE export involve THREE different exchange rates?"

The Mystery

INV-8891 said $90,000. Clean number. Then reality:

  • The bank booked the LC at ৳120.00/$
  • Nasrin's invoice was converted at ৳122.20/$ for customs
  • Sixty days later, when H&M's money actually landed, the bank credited Masco at ৳121.80/$… minus ৳35,000 of bank charges.

Shirin's screen now shows a line called "Foreign Exchange Gain/Loss", and the MD wants to know who lost ৳36,000 and whether Nasrin made an error.

She didn't. Currencies breathe. By module's end you'll read all three rates like a native — and know exactly which one books revenue.

Masco earns USD/EUR from buyers and pays USD for yarn from China, Hong Kong, South Korea — under bond-license back-to-back LCs. Every crossing of the taka border creates accounting events this module explains.

The Lesson: Three Rates, One Truth

The functional currency of Masco's books is BDT. Every dollar event must be translated into taka ON THE DAY IT HAPPENS. But "the day it happens" differs per event — hence three famous rates:

  1. Transaction/booking rate — the rate on the event date. Revenue books at the B/L date's rate; LC liabilities book at their opening date's rate. This is the ONLY rate that creates original entries.
  2. Settlement rate — the rate on the day money actually moves. Almost never equals the booking rate. The gap = realized FX gain or loss, a real profit/loss line.
  3. Closing/assessment rates — used by customs for duty valuation and by accountants at month-end to revalue OPEN foreign-currency balances (unrealized FX until settled).

Back-to-back LC: the RMG engine

Masco rarely holds export risk alone: the buyer's LC (master) lets the bank issue back-to-back import LCs to Chinese yarn suppliers, payable from the export proceeds. Bond license rules let raw materials import duty-free — but every kg is tracked against exports (UD — Utilization Declaration). Accounting must keep USD receivables, USD payables, and their maturities aligned so money arrives before it must leave.

The 120-day rule

Bangladesh Bank requires export proceeds to be realized within a regulatory window (currently ~120 days from shipment). Aging of foreign receivables isn't just commercial hygiene — it's regulatory compliance. Overdue realization = bank explanations, penalties, and audit findings.

There are no dumb questions
Can we just record everything at the invoice rate and forget it?
Then your books claim taka amounts that never arrived. Between booking and settlement the taka MOVES; pretending otherwise fabricates either profit or loss. Honest books revalue open balances monthly and book realized differences at settlement.
Is FX gain "real" profit? Can we pay bonuses from it?
Realized gains (cash actually received vs booked) are genuinely earned financial income. Unrealized month-end revaluations can reverse next month — treat them as valuation noise, not bonus fuel.
Who chooses the daily rate?
Bank-published rates (and Bangladesh Bank references) — pulled into the ERP as dated config tables, NEVER typed ad-hoc. Module 0's Golden Rule applies to rates too: no document/source, no rate.

Exercise 1 — Name that rate

Which rate applies? (booking / settlement / closing)

  1. Booking INV-8891 revenue on B/L date 15 Aug
  2. H&M's USD finally converts to taka on 14 Oct
  3. Month-end revaluation of the still-unpaid H&M receivable on 31 Aug
  4. Customs values imported dyes at landing

Solutions to Exercise 1:

  1. Booking rate (B/L date) — creates the original revenue entry.
  2. Settlement rate — realized FX gain/loss computed vs booking.
  3. Closing rate — unrealized revaluation of open balances.
  4. Customs assessment rate — statutory valuation, may differ from books' own rates.
Exercise 2 — Gain or loss?

Booked H&M receivable $90,000 @ ৳122.20 = ৳1,09,98,000. Settled at ৳121.80. Compute the taka received and state gain or loss.

Solution to Exercise 2:

Received: $90,000 × ৳121.80 = ৳1,09,62,000. Booked was ৳1,09,98,000 → shortfall ৳36,000 = realized FX LOSS. Taka strengthened vs the dollar between August and October, so each dollar bought fewer taka. Nobody's fault — but somebody must RECORD it.

31 Aug — month-end closestill unpaid; closing rate 123.10
14 Oct — cash received@ 121.80, bank charges ৳35,000

Entry 3a — 14 Oct (reverse August's unrealized gain first)

Why reverse? The ৳81,000 gain was a paper valuation at month-end (123.10). It never became cash, and the actual settlement rate (121.80) turned out LOWER than that guess. Honest books undo the estimate before recording reality:

AccountDebit (BDT)Credit (BDT)
FX Gain—Unrealized (undo the estimate)81,000
1310 AR—H&M (back to original booking)81,000

Entry 3b — 14 Oct (settlement at realized rates)

AccountDebit (BDT)Credit (BDT)
1010 Cash at Bank—City Bank ($90,000 @ 121.80)1,09,62,000
Bank Charges35,000
FX Loss—Realized (settling vs booking 122.20)36,000
1310 AR—H&M (original booked balance)1,09,98,000

Follow the receivable across all four entries: booked 1,09,98,000 → revalued to 1,10,79,000 (Aug) → reversal brings it back to 1,09,98,000 → settlement clears it to zero. And the P&L tells one clean story: the August "gain" vanished exactly as it arrived, leaving only the TRUE result of holding dollars for 60 days — a ৳36,000 realized loss. That is the rule in one line: unrealized reverses; realized is forever. The quiz drills this until it's reflex.
