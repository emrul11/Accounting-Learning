Fixed Assets & Depreciation

"A ৳5 crore knitting line earns revenue for ten years. Charging its full cost to one month's tees would be madness — so how does accounting spread it fairly?"

The Mystery

March: Masco buys a new knitting line for ৳5 crore cash. The P&L that month shows… almost no impact from the purchase. The MD fumes: "We spent FIVE CRORE and accounting says nothing happened?!"

Meanwhile the bank statement agrees with him — ৳5 crore gone. And every month for the next decade, a small mysterious expense called "Depreciation — Knitting Machinery" appears, roughly ৳6.9 lakh/month, long after anyone remembers the purchase.

Cash left once; expense arrives monthly. Neither number is a lie. This module explains how both can be true — and why garment costing depends on getting it right.

Fixed assets are production power stored as property. Depreciation is the monthly meter reading of that power being consumed by garments.

The Lesson: Spreading Cost Across the Years It Serves

The matching principle (Module 6!) strikes again: if a machine helps make shirts for ten years, its COST belongs to those same ten years — matched against the revenue it helps earn. Dumping ৳5 crore into March would destroy March's honesty AND April-through-2036's.

Deprecation needs exactly four inputs:

  1. Cost — purchase price + duties + installation + trial-run costs (everything to make it READY)
  2. Useful life — years of service expected (engineering judgment + tax schedules)
  3. Residual value — estimated scrap/sale value at end
  4. Method — straight-line (equal slices) or declining-balance (bigger early slices)
Masco asset classTypical lifeMethod used here
Factory building20–30 yrsStraight-line
Knitting/dyeing machines10–12 yrsStraight-line
Computers & IT3–4 yrsStraight-line

Straight-line in one formula

 Annual depreciation = (Cost − Residual value) ÷ Useful life

 ৳5,00,00,000 knitting line, ৳50L residual, 10 years:
   (5,00,00,000 − 50,00,000) ÷ 10 = ৳45,00,000/year ≈ ৳3,75,000/month
 (Our mystery said ~6.9L? That was TWO lines sharing the story — quiz will ask.)

Where depreciation GOES matters as much as how much

  • Knitting machine depreciation → factory OH pool → into garment cost via machine hours (Module 5)
  • Accounts office computer → admin expense
  • Showroom AC at the Dhaka sales office → selling expense

Same mechanism, three destinations — the payroll fork (Module 8) all over again.

There are no dumb questions
Does depreciation SAVE money for buying a new machine?
No! It's a non-cash expense — pure matching arithmetic. The cash for replacement comes from profits RETAINED and invested, or financing. Confusing the two leaves factories with fully depreciated machines, no replacement fund, and surprised boards.
The machine's market VALUE fell 30% the day we unboxed it. Should depreciation start from that?
Books track COST served over LIFE, not resale mood. Market value matters only at disposal (gain/loss vs book value). Different questions, different numbers.
What happens when we SELL an old machine?
Remove its cost AND accumulated depreciation; whatever cash came in versus remaining BOOK VALUE becomes a gain or loss on disposal — often surprising people who confused book value with market value.
Fireside chat
The Managing Director

So each tee carries a slice of the knitting machine?

Depreciation

Precisely — through the overhead pool and machine-hour rates. Every garment quietly pays rent on the machinery of its birth. Factories that skip this underprice their tees today, then beg banks tomorrow when the machines die with no heirs funded.

The Managing Director

…And the ৳5 crore I watched leave the bank in March?

Depreciation

Real, sir — but that's CASH flow's story (Module 11 separates us properly). Your P&L tells truth across time; your bank account tells truth about now. Both matter. Never let either impersonate the other.

Your Turn

Exercise 1 — Asset, expense, or part of cost?
  1. ৳80,000 paid to ship and install the new knitting line
  2. Monthly electricity of the dyeing hall
  3. ৳35,000 annual software licence for design PCs
  4. Rebuilt the dyeing machine's motor, extending life by 4 years, ৳9L

Solutions:

  1. Capitalized into the asset's cost — needed to make it ready.
  2. Factory overhead expense — running cost, not asset.
  3. Period/admin expense — short-lived right, not a depreciable fixed asset class for us.
  4. Capitalize (major overhaul extending life) — add to asset cost / replace old component; routine servicing would have been expense instead.
Exercise 2 — Compute it

Dyeing machine: cost ৳2,40,00,000, residual ৳20L, straight-line, 11-year life. Annual and monthly depreciation? Which account pair records the monthly entry, and which POOL receives it for costing?

Solution:

(2,40,00,000 − 20,00,000) ÷ 11 = ৳20,00,000/year ≈ ৳1,66,667/month. Entry: Dr Depreciation Expense—Dyeing Machinery / Cr Accumulated Depreciation—Dyeing Machinery. It joins the factory OH pool, then reaches fabric cost via machine-hour application (Module 5's pipes).

After 10 years: fully depreciated, residual ৳50L remains on books until disposal — and the replacement question should have been answered by retained profits YEARS earlier.
