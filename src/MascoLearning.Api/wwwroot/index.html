1. Why This Site Exists

We are building an ERP accounting module for Masco Group. Everyone involved — from commercial officers to software developers — must understand the accounting logic behind the system, not just how to click buttons.

This website is the single source of truth for all accounting rules at Masco. When you have a question about why a GRN creates a journal entry, or when revenue is actually recognized, the answer lives here.

The content is written for people who have never studied accounting. Every concept starts from first principles, using Masco's actual business — knit garments exported to Europe — as the only example context.

Key Takeaway: This site explains why the system works the way it does. If you only learn the clicks, you will break things when exceptions happen.

3. How to Navigate by Role

You do not need to read everything. Start with what touches your daily work, then branch out.

📋

Commercial Officer

LC, Export, Buyer Invoices

Your job and accounting touchpoints: You handle Letters of Credit, prepare export invoices, track buyer payments, and resolve shipping document issues. Every LC opened, every B/L issued, and every buyer claim directly hits the accounting system.

Start here: Module 0 → Module 7 → Module 9 → Module 13

Read later: Modules 5, 6, 8, 10, 11 (for context on how your export revenue connects to production cost and profit)

One thing to watch out for: Revenue is recognized on the B/L date, not when the LC arrives or when cash hits the bank. If you record revenue too early, profit looks inflated. If you delay the B/L, revenue recognition shifts to the wrong month.

🏭

Production / Warehouse Staff

GRN, WIP, Inventory

Your job and accounting touchpoints: You receive yarn, create GRNs, issue materials to production floors, report production output, and move finished goods to the warehouse. Every GRN created, every material issue slip signed, and every production output report triggers an accounting entry.

Start here: Module 0 → Module 5 → Module 6 → Module 13

Read later: Modules 1, 2, 4 (to understand why your operational documents create specific debit and credit entries)

One thing to watch out for: A GRN created 3 days after yarn arrives creates a 3-day gap where inventory exists physically but not in the system. This causes stock-outs in planning and misstated month-end inventory. Timely GRN creation is non-negotiable.

💰

Finance / Accounts Staff

Full Picture

Your job and accounting touchpoints: You see everything. Invoice matching, payment execution, bank reconciliation, month-end closing, tax filings, and financial reporting. You are the final checkpoint before numbers become official.

Start here: Module 0 through Module 14, in order. Do not skip.

Read later: Appendices A–D (reference materials you will use daily once you know the modules)

One thing to watch out for: You will be tempted to "fix" problems by manually adjusting the General Ledger. Never bypass the subledger. If AR subledger shows 8.5M but GL shows 8.2M, the GL is wrong — but you fix it by finding the missing subledger posting, not by typing a manual journal entry. Manual overrides break the audit trail.

👥

HR / Payroll Officer

Salary, Attendance, Compliance

Your job and accounting touchpoints: You manage attendance records, calculate overtime, process monthly payroll, and ensure compliance with Bangladesh Labor Act requirements (festival bonus, WPPF, group insurance). Your salary sheet is the source document for one of the largest expenses in the factory.

Start here: Module 0 → Module 8

Read later: Modules 1, 4, 12 (to understand how payroll entries post automatically and what audit controls apply)

One thing to watch out for: Overtime records must match actual production output. If 50 workers show 30 hours overtime each but production output does not justify the hours, Finance will flag this. Keep attendance, overtime, and production logs consistent and auditable.

💻

Software Developers / Implementers

System Build

Your job and accounting touchpoints: You are building the system that makes all of this automatic. When a warehouse keeper clicks "Approve GRN," your code must create the correct journal entry. When payroll is finalized, your code must split costs between COGS, overhead, and operating expense correctly.

Start here: Read ALL modules for accounting understanding. Start with Module 0 and read through Module 14.

Read later: Technical system specifications are maintained in a separate developer document (database design, API specs, posting engine architecture). This site teaches you what the system must do and why. The developer document teaches you how to build it.

One thing to watch out for: A posting rule that looks correct in isolation can be wrong in sequence. If your code allows invoice matching before GRN approval, the accounting entry will be mathematically valid but logically impossible — and the subledger will never reconcile. Sequence validation is as important as account determination.

5. Important Disclaimers

⚠️ Tax Rates Change Frequently

VAT, TDS, AIT rates, and tax thresholds are set by the National Board of Revenue (NBR) Bangladesh and change with each national budget (typically announced in June every year). Always verify current rates at nbr.gov.bd before implementation. Never hardcode tax rates in any system.

⚠️ Bangladesh Bank Regulations

Regulations on export proceeds realization (currently 120 days from shipment date), repatriation requirements, and LC handling may change. Monitor Bangladesh Bank circulars regularly.

⚠️ RMG-Specific Treatment

This site reflects RMG-specific accounting treatment for Masco Group's knit composite, export-oriented factory context. General accounting textbooks may contradict this — trust this site for Masco context, but consult a licensed Chartered Accountant for statutory audit and tax filing purposes.
